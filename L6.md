# 2. Unit 9 --- Only Time Will Tell

## Main idea: Talking about past, present, and future

### A. Past

Use the simple past for completed situations or facts in the past.

**Examples:**

-   A few years ago, there were fewer students.
-   We visited the city last year.
-   She worked there in 2020.

### B. `Used to`

Use:

> **used to + base verb**

for something that was true or happened regularly in the past but is no
longer true.

**Examples:**

-   We used to hang out after school.
-   I used to play football every weekend.
-   She used to live in Amman.

Meaning:

> In the past, this happened regularly or was true, but now it is
> different.

Do not say:

-   ❌ `I used to playing football.`
-   ❌ `I used to played football.`

Correct:

-   ✅ `I used to play football.`

### C. Present

Use present forms for current situations, habits, or things happening
around now.

**Examples:**

-   These days, they're building new schools.
-   Nowadays, kids meet online.
-   I work with computers.

### D. Future

Common future forms include:

#### `will + base verb`

-   There will be more technology.
-   I will study tonight.

#### `might + base verb`

Use `might` for possibility.

-   Restaurants might not exist in the future.
-   It might rain tomorrow.

#### `be going to + base verb`

Use `be going to` for plans or expected future events.

-   We are going to have a meeting.
-   I'm going to study tonight.

### Unit 9 Formula

``` text
Past:
simple past

Past habit/no longer true:
used to + base verb

Future prediction:
will + base verb

Future possibility:
might + base verb

Future plan/intention:
am/is/are going to + base verb
```

# 3. Unit 10 --- I Like Working with People

## Main idea: Gerunds and short responses

## A. Gerunds

A gerund is:

> **verb + -ing**

used as a noun or after certain expressions.

Common expressions followed by `-ing`:

-   enjoy + V-ing
-   hate + V-ing
-   like + V-ing
-   love + V-ing
-   don't mind + V-ing
-   be good at + V-ing
-   be interested in + V-ing

### Examples

-   I love playing video games.
-   I hate working on weekends.
-   I enjoy learning English.
-   I'm good at solving problems.
-   I don't mind working evenings.
-   I'm interested in learning languages.

### Important

After a preposition such as `at` or `in`, use `-ing`.

-   ✅ I'm good at solving problems.

-   ❌ I'm good at solve problems.

-   ✅ I'm interested in learning languages.

-   ❌ I'm interested in learn languages.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## B. Short Responses

Short responses agree with another person's statement.

The auxiliary verb must match the original sentence.

### Positive statements

**Present simple:**

> I love it. → **So do I.**

**Be:**

> I'm good at it. → **So am I.**

**Modal:**

> I can do it. → **So can I.**

### Negative statements

**Present simple:**

> I don't like it. → **Neither do I.**

**Be:**

> I'm not good at it. → **Neither am I.**

**Modal:**

> I can't stand it. → **Neither can I.**

### How to choose the auxiliary

Look at the verb in the original sentence:

  Original          Response
  ----------------- ----------------
  I like...         So do I.
  I don't like...   Neither do I.
  I am...           So am I.
  I am not...       Neither am I.
  I can...          So can I.
  I can't...        Neither can I.

### Exam tip

Do not automatically use `do`.

The response must match the auxiliary:

-   `am` → `So am I`
-   `can` → `So can I`
-   `do` → `So do I`

# 4. Unit 11 --- It's Really Worth Seeing!

## Main idea: Simple Past Passive

The passive voice focuses on the action or the receiver of the action
rather than the person who performs it.

## Formula

> **Subject + was/were + past participle**

### Active → Passive

Active:

> Walt Disney opened Disneyland in 1955.

Passive:

> Disneyland was opened by Walt Disney in 1955.

### More examples

Active:

> Someone painted the building.

Passive:

> The building was painted.

Active:

> They designed the website.

Passive:

> The website was designed.

Active:

> Someone recorded the song.

Passive:

> The song was recorded.

### `was` or `were`?

Use `was` with singular subjects:

-   The movie **was** released.
-   The building **was** designed.

Use `were` with plural subjects:

-   The movies **were** released.
-   The buildings **were** designed.

### Common past participles

-   paint → painted
-   write → written
-   design → designed
-   win → won
-   record → recorded
-   release → released
-   make → made
-   build → built

## When do we use `by`?

Use `by` when you want to identify who performed the action.

> Disneyland was opened **by Walt Disney**.

If the person is unknown, obvious, or unimportant, `by` can be omitted.

> The window was broken.

# 5. Unit 12 --- It's a Long Story

## Main idea: Past Continuous vs. Simple Past

This distinction is very important.

### Past Continuous

Use:

> **was/were + verb-ing**

for an action that was in progress at a particular time in the past.

Examples:

-   I was having lunch.
-   She was shopping.
-   They were watching TV.

### Simple Past

Use the simple past for a completed event or an event that happened
during the ongoing action.

Examples:

-   I spilled coffee.
-   A celebrity walked into the store.
-   The phone rang.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The common pattern

> **Ongoing action + interruption**

Usually:

> **Past continuous + when + simple past**

Example:

> I was having lunch **when** I spilled coffee.

Another example:

> While I was shopping, a celebrity walked into the store.

Think of it this way:

-   **Background / action in progress** → past continuous
-   **Event that happens / interrupts it** → simple past

### `when`

Often introduces the shorter event:

> I was sleeping **when** the phone rang.

### `while`

Often introduces the longer ongoing action:

> **While** I was sleeping, the phone rang.

### Formula

``` text
was/were + V-ing + when + V2

While + was/were + V-ing, + V2
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Unit 13 --- That's Entertainment!

## Main idea: `-ing` vs. `-ed` adjectives

This is a very common test area.

### `-ing` adjectives

`-ing` describes the thing or person that **causes the feeling**.

Examples:

-   The movie is interesting.
-   The book is fascinating.
-   The movie is boring.
-   The game is exciting.

### `-ed` adjectives

`-ed` describes the person who **experiences the feeling**.

Examples:

-   I'm interested in the movie.
-   I'm fascinated by the book.
-   I am bored by the movie.
-   They are excited about the game.

### The easiest rule

> **-ing = causes the feeling**

> **-ed = experiences the feeling**

### Compare

-   The movie is **boring**.
-   I am **bored**.

The movie causes the boredom.

-   The game is **exciting**.
-   I am **excited**.

The game causes the excitement.

### Common pairs

  `-ing`          `-ed`
  --------------- --------------
  interesting     interested
  boring          bored
  exciting        excited
  fascinating     fascinated
  amazing         amazed
  confusing       confused
  surprising      surprised
  disappointing   disappointed
  frightening     frightened

### Exam trick

Ask:

> Is the subject causing the feeling or experiencing it?

If it causes it → `-ing`.

If it experiences it → `-ed`.

# 7. Unit 14 --- Now I Get It!

## Main idea: Modals + adverbs of probability

## A. Modals

### `may / might`

These indicate possibility.

-   It may rain.
-   She might come.
-   Restaurants might not exist in the future.

### `could`

`Could` can also express possibility.

-   It could mean she doesn't want anything.
-   This could be the answer.

### `must`

`Must` can express a strong conclusion based on evidence.

-   That must mean "no."
-   He must be tired.
-   She must know the answer.

Here, `must` means something like:

> "I am almost certain that this is true."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## B. Adverbs

### `maybe / perhaps`

Indicate possibility.

-   Maybe she is busy.
-   Perhaps he forgot.

### `probably`

Means something is likely.

-   She will probably come.
-   It will probably rain.

### `definitely`

Indicates strong certainty.

-   I will definitely study.
-   He definitely knows the answer.

### Strength

A useful way to remember them:

``` text
maybe / perhaps
      ↓
   possible

probably
      ↓
    likely

definitely / must
      ↓
very strong certainty
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Unit 15 --- I Wouldn't Have Done That

## Main idea: Unreal Conditionals

This unit deserves serious attention.

The main pattern is:

> **If + simple past, would/could/might + base verb**

This is used for hypothetical or unreal situations.

### Examples

> If I found \$40,000, I would keep it.

> If I had more time, I could travel.

> If I won the lottery, I might buy a new car.

### Meaning

The speaker is imagining a situation.

For example:

> If I won the lottery, I would buy a house.

This does not mean that the speaker has won the lottery. It is a
hypothetical situation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## `would`

Use `would` for the imagined result.

> If I had more money, I **would** travel.

## `could`

Use `could` for ability or possibility.

> If I had more money, I **could** travel.

## `might`

Use `might` when the result is possible but uncertain.

> If I had more money, I **might** travel.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Important exam rule

Do **not** normally use `will` in this unreal conditional pattern.

Incorrect:

> ❌ If I found \$40,000, I will buy a car.

Correct:

> ✅ If I found \$40,000, I would buy a car.

### Question form

> What would you do if you found \$40,000?

Possible answer:

> If I found \$40,000, I would save some of it and use the rest to help
> my family.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Unit 16 --- Making Excuses

## Main idea: Reported Speech

Reported speech tells someone what another person said without repeating
the exact original words.

There are two major patterns to know:

1.  Reported statements
2.  Reported requests

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Reported Statements

### Direct speech

> "I'm not feeling well."

### Reported speech

> She said that she wasn't feeling well.

The tense usually moves one step back when the reporting verb is in the
past.

## Important changes

  Direct     Reported
  ---------- ----------
  am/is      was
  are        were
  have/has   had
  made       had made
  will       would
  can        could
  may        might

### Examples

Direct:

> "I am tired."

Reported:

> He said that he was tired.

Direct:

> "I have a car."

Reported:

> She said that she had a car.

Direct:

> "We will be out of town."

Reported:

> They said that they would be out of town.

Direct:

> "I can help."

Reported:

> He said that he could help.

Direct:

> "It may rain."

Reported:

> She said that it might rain.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. `Said` vs. `Told`

This is important.

## `say`

Usually:

> **say + statement**

Example:

> She said that she was tired.

Do not normally use a person directly after `say` in this pattern:

-   ❌ She said me that she was tired.
-   ✅ She said that she was tired.
-   ✅ She said to me that she was tired.

## `tell`

Use:

> **tell + person + statement**

Example:

> She told me that she was tired.

Compare:

> She said that she was tired.

> She told me that she was tired.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Reported Requests

Reported requests have a different structure.

## Positive request

Direct:

> "Arrive on time."

Reported:

> She told me to arrive on time.

### Formula

> **told + person + to + base verb**

Examples:

-   He told me to wait.
-   She told him to call her.
-   They told us to arrive early.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Negative request

Direct:

> "Don't leave your bike here."

Reported:

> He told me not to leave my bike there.

### Formula

> **told + person + not to + base verb**

Examples:

-   She told me not to worry.
-   He told us not to leave.
-   They told him not to touch it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Requests with `ask`

Direct:

> "Can you pick up some food?"

Reported:

> She asked me to pick up some food.

### Formula

> **asked + person + to + base verb**

Examples:

-   She asked me to help.
-   He asked her to wait.
-   They asked us to arrive early.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Difference Between `say`, `tell`, and `ask`

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Verb                    Pattern                 Example
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  say                     said + statement        She said that she was
                                                  tired.

  tell                    told + person +         She told me that she
                          statement/request       was tired.

  ask                     asked + person + to +   She asked me to help.
                          verb                    
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Remember:

> **say → statement**

> **tell → person + information/instruction**

> **ask → person + request**

## Unit 9 --- "Fighting for a Future"

The reading is about Aguaviva, a small village in Spain whose population
was declining.

Mayor Luis Bricio tried to attract immigrant families by offering:

-   homes
-   jobs
-   health care
-   education

Afterward, the village gained:

-   more students
-   more workers
-   more economic activity

However, the new families also experienced problems and challenges.

### Questions you should be able to answer

1.  Why were young people leaving?
2.  What did Luis Bricio do?
3.  What changed after 2000?
4.  What benefits resulted?
5.  What problems did the new families experience?

For any reading passage, practice answering:

> Who?

> What?

> Why?

> When?

> What changed?

> What was the result?

# 22. Final Study Strategy

The most efficient approach is:

### Step 1 --- Memorize the formulas

Learn the structures first.

### Step 2 --- Understand the meaning

Do not memorize grammar mechanically. Know why a form is used.

### Step 3 --- Practice transformation

Especially practice:

-   active → passive
-   direct → reported speech
-   direct requests → reported requests
-   positive → short agreement
-   incomplete sentences → correct conditional

### Step 4 --- Practice choosing between similar forms

Examples:

-   `used to` vs. present
-   `was doing` vs. `did`
-   `interesting` vs. `interested`
-   `will` vs. `might` vs. `going to`
-   `said` vs. `told` vs. `asked`
-   `would` vs. `could` vs. `might`

### Step 5 --- Review vocabulary

Focus on collocations and phrases.

### Step 6 --- Test yourself without notes

If you can produce the grammar correctly without looking at the
formulas, you are much more likely to be ready for the exam.

## Golden Rule

When solving grammar questions, first identify the **purpose** of the
sentence:

-   Past habit → `used to`
-   Future prediction → `will`
-   Future possibility → `might`
-   Future plan → `going to`
-   Verb after certain expressions/prepositions → `V-ing`
-   Action received by subject → passive
-   Ongoing past action → past continuous
-   Completed/interruption event → simple past
-   Cause of feeling → `-ing`
-   Person experiencing feeling → `-ed`
-   Possibility → `may/might/could`
-   Strong conclusion → `must`
-   Hypothetical situation → unreal conditional
-   Repeating what someone said → reported speech
-   Reporting an instruction/request → `told/asked + person + to + verb`
