## 2. Know These Grammar Patterns Cold

### Unit 9

**Past:**

- A few years ago, there were...
- We used to go...

**Present:**

- These days, they're building...
- Nowadays, kids meet online.

**Future:**

- There will be...
- Restaurants might not exist.
- We are going to have...

Pay particular attention to **used to + base verb**:

> We used to hang out after school.

It describes something that was true/habitual in the past but is no longer true.

### Unit 10

#### Gerunds

- I love playing video games.
- I hate working on weekends.
- I'm good at solving problems.
- I don't mind working evenings.
- I'm interested in learning languages.

Remember that after these expressions you use **verb + -ing**.

#### Short Responses

**Positive:**

- I love it. → **So do I.**
- I'm good at it. → **So am I.**
- I can do it. → **So can I.**

**Negative:**

- I don't like it. → **Neither do I.**
- I'm not good at it. → **Neither am I.**
- I can't stand it. → **Neither can I.**

This is worth practicing separately because the auxiliary changes depending on the original sentence.

### Unit 11

#### Simple Past Passive

**Active:** Walt Disney opened Disneyland in 1955.

**Passive:** Disneyland was opened by Walt Disney in 1955.

**Formula:**

> Subject + was/were + past participle

Examples:

- was painted
- was written
- was designed
- was won
- was recorded
- was released

You should be able to convert **active → passive** and recognize passive sentences.

### Unit 12

This distinction is important:

**Past continuous = action in progress**

> was/were + verb-ing

**Simple past = completed/interruption**

Example:

> I was having lunch when I spilled coffee.

Another:

> While I was shopping, a celebrity walked into the store.

Think:

> **background/ongoing action → past continuous**  
> **event that happens/interupts it → simple past**

### Unit 13

This is a very common test area:

**-ing describes the thing/person that causes the feeling.**

- The movie is interesting.
- The book is fascinating.
- The movie is boring.

**-ed describes the person who experiences the feeling.**

- I'm interested in the movie.
- I'm fascinated by the book.
- I'm bored by the movie.

Don't confuse:

> The movie is boring.  
> I am bored.

The movie causes the feeling; I experience it.

### Unit 14

Know the strength/meaning of these:

- **may/might** = possibility
- **could** = possibility
- **must** = strong conclusion/certainty

Examples:

- It might mean she wants to accept it.
- It could mean she doesn't want anything.
- That must mean "no."

**Adverbs:**

- **maybe / perhaps** → possibility
- **probably** → likely
- **definitely** → strong certainty

### Unit 15

This one deserves serious attention.

#### Unreal Conditional

> **If + simple past, would/could/might + base verb**

Examples:

- If I found $40,000, I would keep it.
- If I had more time, I could travel.
- If I won the lottery, I might buy a new car.

Question:

> What would you do if you found $40,000?

Do not use **will** in the main pattern:

- ❌ If I found $40,000, I will buy a car.
- ✅ If I found $40,000, I would buy a car.

## 3. Unit 16 Is Especially Important Because Page 54 Is Inside It

There are two reported-speech structures you need to distinguish.

### Reported Statements

**Direct:**

> "I'm not feeling well."

**Reported:**

> She said that she wasn't feeling well.

Important changes shown in the PDF include:

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| am/is → | was |
| have → | had |
| made → | had made |
| will → | would |
| can → | could |
| may → | might |

Example:

> "We will be out of town."  
> They said they would be out of town.

### Reported Requests

This is another major pattern:

> **said/told + person + to + verb**

"Arrive on time."

> She told me to arrive on time.

**Negative:**

"Don't leave your bike here."

> He told me not to leave my bike there.

**Question/request:**

"Can you pick up some food?"

> She asked me to pick up some food.

Know the difference:

- **say** → said + statement
- **tell** → told + person + statement/request
- **ask** → asked + person + to + verb

For example:

- She said to arrive on time.
- She told me to arrive on time.
- She asked me to arrive on time.

The reported requests on **page 50** and reported statements on **page 52** are particularly important.

## 5. Readings

Don't memorize the reading passages word-for-word. Know the main idea, important facts, causes, consequences, and vocabulary.

For example, Unit 9's reading **"Fighting for a Future"** is about Aguaviva, a small village in Spain whose population was declining. Mayor Luis Bricio tried to attract immigrant families by offering homes, jobs, health care, and education. The village subsequently gained students, workers, and economic activity.

For reading questions, be able to answer:

- Why were young people leaving?
- What did Luis Bricio do?
- What changed after 2000?
- What benefits resulted?
- What problems did the new families experience?
