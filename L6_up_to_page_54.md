# UNIT 9 — ONLY TIME WILL TELL

## Main idea

Unit 9 is about **changes over time**:

- the past
- the present
- the future
- consequences of changes

The most important grammar point is **time contrasts**, especially `used to`.

---

## 1. Past

Use past forms to describe things that were true or happened before now.

Examples:

> A few years ago, there were just houses here.

> We used to go to a burger place after class every day.

### `Used to`

### Formula

```text
used to + base verb
```

Examples:

> I used to play outside every day.

> We used to hang out after school.

> People used to shop at small grocery stores.

### Meaning

`Used to` describes a **past habit or past situation that is no longer true**.

Compare:

> I used to live in Amman.

This suggests I lived there before, but I do not live there now.

> I lived in Amman in 2018.

This simply tells us about the past. It does not necessarily emphasize a change.

### Important

After `used to`, use the **base form**:

- used to go
- used to live
- used to play
- used to have

Not:

- ❌ used to went
- ❌ used to living
- ❌ used to played

---

## 2. Present

The unit uses expressions such as:

- these days
- today
- nowadays

Examples:

> These days, they're building lots of apartments.

> Today, people order food from their phones.

> Nowadays, kids only meet online.

These expressions tell us that the situation is true **now or around the present time**.

---

## 3. Future

The unit introduces several ways to talk about the future.

### `will + base verb`

Use `will` for a future prediction.

> There will be apartment blocks everywhere.

### `might + base verb`

Use `might` when the future is possible but uncertain.

> Restaurants might not exist.

### `be going to + base verb`

Use `be going to` for a future plan, intention, or expected development.

> We are going to have virtual friends.

### Compare

```text
will       = prediction
might      = possible prediction
going to   = planned/intended or expected future
```

---

## 4. Time contrast

A useful way to organize Unit 9 is:

```text
PAST       → PRESENT       → FUTURE

used to      these days      will
a few years  nowadays        might
ago          today           going to
```

Example:

> In the past, kids used to hang out with friends after school.

> Nowadays, kids meet online.

> In the future, students might study from anywhere.

---

## 5. Consequences

Unit 9 also practices **cause and effect**.

Example:

> If you get a job, you may spend less time at the gym.

This means:

```text
cause:     get a job
result:    spend less time at the gym
```

Another:

> If you spend less time at the gym, you won't keep in shape.

The important skill is recognizing how one event can lead to another.

# UNIT 10 — I LIKE WORKING WITH PEOPLE

## Main idea

Unit 10 is about:

- jobs
- workplace skills
- things people like or dislike doing
- gerunds
- agreeing and disagreeing with short responses

The most important grammar is **gerunds**.

---

# 1. Gerunds

A gerund is:

```text
verb + ing
```

It can function as the activity being discussed.

Examples:

> I love playing video games.

> I hate working on weekends.

> I'm good at solving problems.

> I don't mind working evenings.

> I'm interested in learning languages.

---

## 2. Expressions followed by `-ing`

Learn these as fixed patterns:

```text
enjoy + V-ing
hate + V-ing
like + V-ing
love + V-ing
don't mind + V-ing
be good at + V-ing
be interested in + V-ing
```

Examples:

> I enjoy working with people.

> I don't mind working on weekends.

> She's good at solving problems.

> He's interested in learning languages.

### Very important

After a preposition, use `-ing`.

For example:

```text
good at + V-ing
interested in + V-ing
```

Correct:

> I'm good at solving problems.

> I'm interested in learning languages.

Incorrect:

> ❌ I'm good at solve problems.

> ❌ I'm interested in learn languages.

---

# 3. Short responses

The unit teaches short responses for agreeing or disagreeing.

The key is that the auxiliary verb must match the first sentence.

## Positive agreement

> I love playing video games.

> **So do I.**

Why `do`?

Because the original sentence uses a normal verb: `love`.

---

> I'm good at solving problems.

> **So am I.**

Why `am`?

Because the original sentence uses `be`.

---

> I can do it.

> **So can I.**

Why `can`?

Because the original sentence uses the modal `can`.

---

## Negative agreement

> I don't like it.

> **Neither do I.**

> I'm not good at selling.

> **Neither am I.**

> I can't stand commuting.

> **Neither can I.**

---

## The rule to memorize

```text
do  → So do I / Neither do I
be  → So am I / Neither am I
can → So can I / Neither can I
```

The same idea works with other auxiliaries too.

### Do not choose the response randomly.

Look at the auxiliary in the original sentence.

# UNIT 11 — IT'S REALLY WORTH SEEING!

## Main idea

Unit 11 focuses on:

- landmarks
- famous works
- facts about famous places
- the **simple past passive**

---

# 1. Passive voice

The passive changes the **focus** of the sentence.

### Active

> The Disney brothers founded the company in 1923.

Focus = **the Disney brothers**, the people who did the action.

### Passive

> It was founded by the Disney brothers in 1923.

Focus = **the company**, the thing affected by the action.

---

# 2. Simple past passive formula

```text
was/were + past participle
```

Examples:

> was founded

> was opened

> was broadcast

> was designed

> was written

> was recorded

> was released

---

# 3. Active → passive

Start with:

> Walt Disney opened Disneyland in 1955.

### Step 1

Find the object:

> Disneyland

### Step 2

Move it to the beginning:

> Disneyland...

### Step 3

Use past `be`:

> Disneyland was...

### Step 4

Use the past participle:

> Disneyland was opened...

### Step 5

Add the original subject with `by` if it is important:

> Disneyland was opened **by Walt Disney** in 1955.

---

## Formula

```text
Active:
Subject + past verb + object

Passive:
Object + was/were + past participle + by + subject
```

Example:

> J. K. Rowling wrote the first Harry Potter book.

→

> The first Harry Potter book was written by J. K. Rowling.

---

# 4. When to use `by`

Use `by + person/agent` when you want to say **who performed the action**.

> The building was designed **by William Lamb**.

But the agent is sometimes unnecessary.

> The building was designed in 1931.

The important information may simply be the building and the action.

---

# 5. Common past participles

Memorize common irregular forms:

```text
write  → written
win    → won
see    → seen
give   → given
make   → made
build  → built
choose → chosen
```

Regular verbs normally use `-ed`:

```text
design   → designed
record   → recorded
release  → released
open     → opened
```

# UNIT 12 — IT'S A LONG STORY

## Main idea

Unit 12 is about:

- telling stories
- describing events
- explaining what was happening when something else occurred
- **past continuous vs. simple past**

---

# 1. Past continuous

Use the past continuous for an action that was **in progress at a particular time in the past**.

### Formula

```text
was/were + verb-ing
```

Examples:

> I was having lunch.

> I was driving to the airport.

> She was shopping.

---

# 2. Simple past

Use the simple past for a completed event.

Examples:

> I spilled coffee.

> I got a flat tire.

> A celebrity walked into the store.

---

# 3. The important contrast

The textbook's main idea is:

```text
past continuous = action in progress
simple past     = action that interrupts it
```

Example:

> I was having lunch when I spilled coffee on my clothes.

Timeline:

```text
I was having lunch
───────────────────────────
                 X
            spilled coffee
```

The lunch was already happening.

Then the coffee-spilling event occurred.

---

## Another example

> I was driving to the airport, but I got a flat tire.

```text
was driving = ongoing background action
got a flat tire = completed event
```

---

## With `while`

`while` commonly introduces the longer action.

> While I was shopping one day, a celebrity walked into the store.

```text
While + past continuous → simple past
```

---

# 4. How to choose the tense in an exam

Ask:

### Was the action already happening?

Use:

> `was/were + V-ing`

### Did something happen during it?

Use:

> simple past

Example:

> I ________ (drive) when I ________ (realize) I was on the wrong side of the road.

Answer:

> I **was driving** when I **realized** I was on the wrong side of the road.

---

# 5. Unit 12 reading

For reading questions, focus on:

- what happened
- what caused it
- what the people experienced
- the sequence of events
- the main point of the article

The important exam skill is not memorizing the whole passage. It is being able to locate the relevant fact and understand the sequence.

# UNIT 13 — THAT'S ENTERTAINMENT!

## Main idea

---

# 1. `-ing` vs. `-ed` adjectives

This is one of the most important distinctions in the material.

## `-ing`

The `-ing` adjective describes the **thing/person that causes the feeling**.

Examples:

> The movie is interesting.

> The book is fascinating.

> The movie is boring.

> The game is exciting.

---

## `-ed`

The `-ed` adjective describes the **person who experiences the feeling**.

Examples:

> I'm interested in the movie.

> I'm fascinated by the book.

> I'm bored by the movie.

> We're excited about the game.

---

# 2. The easiest way to remember it

Ask:

### What causes the feeling?

Use `-ing`.

> The movie is **boring**.

### Who feels it?

Use `-ed`.

> I am **bored**.

---

## More examples

```text
The movie is exciting.
I am excited.

The book is fascinating.
I am fascinated.

The story is confusing.
I am confused.

The news is shocking.
I am shocked.

The situation is embarrassing.
I am embarrassed.
```

---

# 3. Common mistake

Do not say:

> ❌ I am boring.

unless you literally mean **you cause other people to feel bored**.

Normally, if you mean that you feel bored, say:

> I am bored.

Similarly:

> The movie is interesting.

not:

> ❌ The movie is interested.

# UNIT 14 — NOW I GET IT!

## Main idea

Unit 14 deals with:

- gestures
- communication
- understanding meanings
- making conclusions
- **modals and adverbs**

---

# 1. Modals of possibility and certainty

## `may / might`

These express **possibility**.

> It might mean she wants to accept it.

> It may mean she wants to accept it.

Meaning:

> There is a possibility, but I am not certain.

---

## `could`

`Could` can also express possibility.

> It could mean she doesn't want anything.

Meaning:

> This is one possible explanation.

---

## `must`

`Must` expresses a **strong conclusion**.

> That must mean “no.”

The speaker believes the evidence makes the conclusion very likely or certain.

---

# 2. Adverbs

The unit contrasts several adverbs.

### Maybe / perhaps

These indicate possibility.

> Maybe it means she wants to accept it.

> Perhaps it means she doesn't want anything.

### Probably

This means **likely**.

> It probably means she doesn't want anything.

### Definitely

This indicates strong certainty.

> That definitely means “no.”

---

# 3. Strength scale

A useful exam memory system is:

```text
maybe / perhaps
       ↓
   may / might
       ↓
      could
       ↓
   probably
       ↓
   definitely
```

However, do not treat this as a perfect mathematical scale. The exact strength depends on context.

The main distinction is:

```text
possibility → likelihood → strong certainty
```

---

# 4. Communication and gestures

The unit also shows that gestures can have different meanings in different cultures.

For example, the same movement may communicate different ideas depending on the country or culture.

Therefore:

> Do not automatically assume that a gesture has one universal meaning.

This connects directly to the grammar because you may need to make a **guess** about what a gesture means:

> It might mean...

> It could mean...

> It probably means...

> It must mean...

---

# 5. Unit 14 reading — “Understanding Idioms”

The reading focuses on **idioms**, expressions whose meanings cannot always be understood literally from the individual words.

For example, an idiom may have a figurative meaning that differs from its literal meaning.

For reading questions:

- identify the idiom
- identify its meaning
- use the surrounding context
- do not interpret every expression literally

# UNIT 15 — I WOULDN'T HAVE DONE THAT

## Main idea

Unit 15 deals with:

- difficult situations
- advice and possible consequences
- hypothetical situations
- **unreal conditional sentences**

This is one of the most important grammar areas.

---

# 1. Unreal conditional

### Formula

```text
If + simple past,
would/could/might + base verb
```

Example:

> If I found $40,000, I would keep it.

The situation is imaginary/hypothetical.

---

# 2. `Would`

Use `would` for a hypothetical result.

> If I won the lottery, I would buy a house.

Meaning:

> I am imagining that I win the lottery and describing my result.

---

# 3. `Could`

Use `could` for a possible ability, opportunity, or result.

> If I had more time, I could travel.

Meaning:

> Having more time would make travel possible.

---

# 4. `Might`

Use `might` when the hypothetical result is possible but uncertain.

> If I won the lottery, I might buy a new car.

Meaning:

> Buying the car is one possible result.

---

# 5. Very important: do not use `will`

For the pattern taught in Unit 15:

❌

> If I found $40,000, I will buy a car.

Correct:

> If I found $40,000, I would buy a car.

---

# 6. More examples

> If you lost your sister's favorite sweater, what would you do?

> If you had three months to travel, where would you go?

> If your doctor told you to exercise more, which activity would you choose?

> If you saw your favorite movie star, what would you do?

---

# 7. Word order

You can put the `if` clause first or second.

> If I found $40,000, I would keep it.

or:

> I would keep it if I found $40,000.

When the `if` clause comes first, normally use a comma.

---

# 8. How to recognize this grammar in an exam

Look for:

- `if`
- an imaginary or hypothetical situation
- simple past after `if`
- `would`, `could`, or `might` in the result

Pattern:

```text
IF + V2 → WOULD/COULD/MIGHT + base verb
```

Example:

> If I **had** more money, I **would travel** more.

# UNIT 16 — MAKING EXCUSES

## Main idea

Unit 16 covers:

- excuses
- requests
- apologies
- complaints
- reasons
- reported speech

The two major grammar sections are:

1. **Reported speech: requests**
2. **Reported speech: statements**

Both are highly important.

---

# 1. Reported requests

A reported request tells another person what someone asked or told you to do.

---

## A. `tell + person + to + verb`

Original request:

> Arrive on time for the meeting.

Reported:

> She told me to arrive on time for the meeting.

### Formula

```text
told + person + to + base verb
```

Examples:

> He told me to wait.

> She told us to be careful.

> They told him to call later.

---

# 2. Negative reported requests

Original:

> Don't leave your bike in the hallway.

Reported:

> He told me not to leave my bike in the hallway.

### Formula

```text
told + person + not to + base verb
```

Examples:

> She told me not to worry.

> He told us not to arrive late.

---

# 3. `ask + person + to + verb`

A request using `Can you...?` can become:

> She asked me to pick up some food.

### Formula

```text
asked + person + to + base verb
```

Example:

Direct:

> Can you help me?

Reported:

> She asked me to help her.

---

# 4. `say`

The book also demonstrates `say` with requests.

Example:

> She said to arrive on time for the meeting.

For exam purposes, the safest core patterns to memorize are:

```text
said + statement

told + person + statement/request

asked + person + to + verb
```

---

# 5. Say vs. tell vs. ask

This distinction is extremely important.

## SAY

```text
say + statement
```

> She said that she was tired.

## TELL

```text
tell + person + statement
tell + person + to + verb
```

> She told me that she was tired.

> She told me to wait.

## ASK

```text
ask + person + to + verb
```

> She asked me to wait.

---

# 6. Reported statements

Reported speech tells us what someone said without using their exact original words.

### Direct

> “I'm not feeling well.”

### Reported

> She said that she wasn't feeling well.

Notice the tense change:

```text
am → was
```

---

# 7. Important tense changes

The unit gives these major changes:

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
|---|---|
| am/is | was |
| have | had |
| made | had made |
| have planned | had planned |
| can | could |
| will | would |
| may | might |

---

## Examples

### `am → was`

Direct:

> “I'm not feeling well.”

Reported:

> She said that she wasn't feeling well.

---

### `have → had`

Direct:

> “I have houseguests for the weekend.”

Reported:

> She said that she had houseguests for the weekend.

---

### `made → had made`

Direct:

> “I made a tennis date with Kim.”

Reported:

> She said that she had made a tennis date with Kim.

---

### `will → would`

Direct:

> “We will be out of town.”

Reported:

> They said that they would be out of town.

---

### `can → could`

Direct:

> “We can't come tomorrow.”

Reported:

> They told me that they couldn't come tomorrow.

---

### `may → might`

Direct:

> “We may go out with friends.”

Reported:

> They said that they might go out with friends.

---

# 8. The basic reported-statement formula

```text
Subject + said/told + (that) + reported clause
```

Examples:

> She said that she was tired.

> They told me that they couldn't come.

The word `that` is often optional in everyday English:

> She said that she was tired.

> She said she was tired.

---

# 9. Why the tense changes

When the reporting verb is in the past (`said`, `told`), English commonly shifts the original tense backward.

Think:

```text
Direct                 Reported

am/is        →         was
have         →         had
can          →         could
will         →         would
may          →         might
```

This is often called **backshift**.

# The 10 Patterns You Should Know Cold

```text
1. used to + base verb

2. enjoy/hate/like/love/don't mind + V-ing

3. be good at + V-ing
   be interested in + V-ing

4. So do I / So am I / So can I
   Neither do I / Neither am I / Neither can I

5. was/were + past participle

6. was/were + V-ing
   + simple past interruption

7. V-ing = causes feeling
   V-ed = experiences feeling

8. may/might/could = possibility
   must = strong conclusion

9. If + simple past,
   would/could/might + base verb

10. Reported speech:
    said/told + clause
    told + person + to + verb
    told + person + not to + verb
    asked + person + to + verb
```

## Final memory trick

Think of the eight units as a progression:

```text
UNIT 9
TIME
Past → Present → Future

UNIT 10
ACTIVITIES
Verb → V-ing

UNIT 11
ACTIONS
Active → Passive

UNIT 12
STORIES
Ongoing → Interrupted

UNIT 13
FEELINGS
Causing → Experiencing
-ing → -ed

UNIT 14
MEANING
Possible → Likely → Certain

UNIT 15
IMAGINATION
If + past → would/could/might

UNIT 16
REPORTING
Direct speech → Reported speech
```

If you can reproduce these patterns accurately and recognize them in exercises, you have covered the central grammar progression in **PDF pages 1–54**.
