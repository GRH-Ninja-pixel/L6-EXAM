# 2. Grammar Patterns You Need to Know Cold

## Unit 9 — Time Contrasts

### Past

Examples:

> A few years ago, there were...

> We used to go...

### Present

Examples:

> These days, they're building...

> Nowadays, kids meet online.

### Future

Examples:

> There will be...

> Restaurants might not exist.

> We are going to have...

### Used to

**Formula:**

```text
used to + base verb
```

Example:

> We used to hang out after school.

**Meaning:** Something was true or happened regularly in the past, but it is no longer true or does not happen regularly now.

Another example:

> I used to play football every weekend.

This means I regularly played football in the past, but I do not necessarily do that now.

# Unit 10 — Gerunds + Short Responses

## Gerunds

A gerund is a verb ending in **-ing** that functions as a noun after certain expressions.

Examples:

> I love playing video games.

> I hate working on weekends.

> I'm good at solving problems.

> I don't mind working evenings.

> I'm interested in learning languages.

### Important Pattern

After these expressions, use:

```text
verb + -ing
```

Common expressions:

```text
enjoy + V-ing
hate + V-ing
like + V-ing
don't mind + V-ing
be good at + V-ing
be interested in + V-ing
```

Examples:

> I enjoy reading.

> She is good at solving problems.

> They are interested in learning languages.

---

## Short Responses

The auxiliary verb in the response must match the original sentence.

### Positive

> I love it. → **So do I.**

> I'm good at it. → **So am I.**

> I can do it. → **So can I.**

### Negative

> I don't like it. → **Neither do I.**

> I'm not good at it. → **Neither am I.**

> I can't stand it. → **Neither can I.**

### Key Rule

```text
do → So do I / Neither do I
be → So am I / Neither am I
can → So can I / Neither can I
```

Do not automatically use **do**. Look at the auxiliary verb in the original sentence.

# Unit 11 — Simple Past Passive

## Formula

```text
Subject + was/were + past participle
```

### Active

> Walt Disney opened Disneyland in 1955.

### Passive

> Disneyland was opened by Walt Disney in 1955.

The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.

### Common Past Participles

```text
painted
written
designed
won
recorded
released
built
created
invented
```

### When to Use Passive

Use the passive when the action or result is more important than who performed the action.

Example:

> The movie was released in 2020.

The person who released it is not important in this sentence.

### Using "by"

Use **by + person/organization** when you want to identify who performed the action.

> The painting was created **by Leonardo da Vinci**.

# Unit 12 — Past Continuous vs. Simple Past

This distinction is very important.

## Past Continuous

Used for an action that was in progress.

```text
was/were + verb-ing
```

Examples:

> I was studying.

> They were watching TV.

> She was shopping.

## Simple Past

Used for a completed event or an event that interrupts another action.

Examples:

> I studied.

> They watched TV.

> She walked into the store.

---

## Together

Think:

```text
background / ongoing action → past continuous
interrupting event → simple past
```

Example:

> I was having lunch when I spilled coffee.

**was having** = action already in progress

**spilled** = event that happened during it

Another example:

> While I was shopping, a celebrity walked into the store.

**was shopping** = ongoing action

**walked** = completed event

### Common Pattern

```text
was/were + V-ing + when + V2
```

Example:

> I was sleeping when the phone rang.

You can also use **while**:

> While I was sleeping, the phone rang.

# Unit 13 — Participles as Adjectives

This is a very common test area.

## -ing Adjectives

**-ing** describes the thing or person that **causes the feeling**.

Examples:

> The movie is interesting.

> The book is fascinating.

> The movie is boring.

> The class is exciting.

## -ed Adjectives

**-ed** describes the person who **experiences the feeling**.

Examples:

> I'm interested in the movie.

> I'm fascinated by the book.

> I am bored by the movie.

> We are excited about the trip.

### Easy Rule

```text
-ing = causes the feeling
-ed = experiences the feeling
```

Compare:

> The movie is **boring**.

The movie causes the feeling.

> I am **bored**.

I experience the feeling.

### Common Pairs

```text
interesting → interested
boring → bored
exciting → excited
fascinating → fascinated
amusing → amused
confusing → confused
surprising → surprised
frightening → frightened
```

# Unit 14 — Modals + Adverbs

## Modal Verbs

### May / Might

Indicate possibility.

> It may mean she wants to accept it.

> It might mean she wants to accept it.

### Could

Can also indicate possibility.

> It could mean she doesn't want anything.

### Must

Indicates a strong conclusion or high certainty.

> That must mean "no."

### General Strength

```text
may / might / could → possibility
must → strong conclusion / strong certainty
```

---

## Adverbs

### Maybe / Perhaps

Possibility:

> Maybe she is busy.

> Perhaps he forgot.

### Probably

Likely:

> She will probably come.

### Definitely

Strong certainty:

> He will definitely arrive.

### General Strength

```text
maybe / perhaps → possible
probably → likely
definitely → very certain
```

# Unit 15 — Unreal Conditionals

This unit deserves serious attention.

## Formula

```text
If + simple past,
would/could/might + base verb
```

Examples:

> If I found $40,000, I would keep it.

> If I had more time, I could travel.

> If I won the lottery, I might buy a new car.

### Question Pattern

> What would you do if you found $40,000?

### Important Rule

Do **not** normally use **will** in this unreal conditional pattern.

Incorrect:

> ❌ If I found $40,000, I will buy a car.

Correct:

> ✅ If I found $40,000, I would buy a car.

### Meanings

**would** = imagined result

> If I had more money, I would travel.

**could** = imagined ability/possibility

> If I had more money, I could travel.

**might** = possible imagined result

> If I had more money, I might travel.

# Unit 16 — Reported Speech

Unit 16 is especially important.

You need to distinguish between:

1. **Reported statements**
2. **Reported requests**

---

## A. Reported Statements

### Direct Speech

> "I'm not feeling well."

### Reported Speech

> She said that she wasn't feeling well.

The tense often moves one step into the past.

### Important Changes

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| am/is | was |
| are | were |
| have/has | had |
| made | had made |
| will | would |
| can | could |
| may | might |

### Examples

Direct:

> "I am tired."

Reported:

> He said that he was tired.

Direct:

> "We will be out of town."

Reported:

> They said they would be out of town.

Direct:

> "I can help."

Reported:

> She said that she could help.

Direct:

> "It may rain."

Reported:

> He said that it might rain.

---

## B. Reported Requests

This structure is different from reported statements.

### Positive Request

```text
told + person + to + verb
```

Direct:

> "Arrive on time."

Reported:

> She told me to arrive on time.

### Negative Request

```text
told + person + not to + verb
```

Direct:

> "Don't leave your bike here."

Reported:

> He told me not to leave my bike there.

### Request with Ask

```text
asked + person + to + verb
```

Direct:

> "Can you pick up some food?"

Reported:

> She asked me to pick up some food.

---

## Say vs. Tell vs. Ask

This distinction is important.

### Say

Use **say/said** for a statement.

> She said that she was tired.

### Tell

Use **tell/told + person**.

> She told me that she was tired.

For requests:

> She told me to arrive on time.

### Ask

Use **ask/asked + person** for a request.

> She asked me to arrive on time.

### Quick Comparison

```text
say → said + statement

tell → told + person + statement/request

ask → asked + person + to + verb
```

Remember:

> ❌ She told to arrive on time.

> ✅ She told **me** to arrive on time.

# 4. Reading Comprehension

Do not memorize reading passages word-for-word.

Instead, know:

```text
main idea
important facts
causes
consequences
key vocabulary
```

## Unit 9 — "Fighting for a Future"

The reading is about **Aguaviva**, a small village in Spain whose population was declining.

Mayor **Luis Bricio** tried to attract immigrant families by offering:

```text
homes
jobs
health care
education
```

The village subsequently gained:

```text
students
workers
economic activity
```

### Questions You Should Be Able to Answer

1. Why were young people leaving?
2. What did Luis Bricio do?
3. What changed after 2000?
4. What benefits resulted?
5. What problems did the new families experience?

For reading questions, focus on **who, what, why, when, and what happened as a result**.

# 7. Final Exam Strategy

When answering grammar questions, first identify the **time**, **structure**, or **function** being tested.

Ask yourself:

```text
Is this past, present, or future?
Is this an ongoing action or a completed event?
Is this active or passive?
Does this verb require -ing?
Does the adjective cause the feeling or experience it?
Is this possibility or certainty?
Is this an unreal condition?
Is this direct or reported speech?
Is this a statement or a request?
```

Then apply the formula.

The most important formulas to memorize are:

```text
was/were + past participle
was/were + V-ing
If + simple past → would/could/might + base verb
told + person + to + verb
asked + person + to + verb
said + clause
V-ing = causes feeling
V-ed = experiences feeling
```

For the exam, understanding these patterns is more useful than memorizing isolated sentences.
