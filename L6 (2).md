# 2. Grammar Patterns to Know Cold

## Unit 9 --- Past, Present, Future & Used To

### Past

-   A few years ago, there were...
-   We used to go...

### Present

-   These days, they're building...
-   Nowadays, kids meet online.

### Future

-   There will be...
-   Restaurants might not exist.
-   We are going to have...

### Used to

**Formula:**

`used to + base verb`

Example:

> We used to hang out after school.

**Meaning:** Something was true or habitual in the past but is no longer
true.

### Future forms

-   `will + base verb`
-   `might + base verb`
-   `be going to + base verb`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Unit 10 --- Gerunds & Short Responses

### Gerunds

A **gerund** is a verb ending in `-ing` that functions as a noun or
follows certain expressions.

Use **verb + -ing** after:

-   enjoy
-   hate
-   like
-   love
-   don't mind
-   be good at
-   be interested in

Examples:

-   I love **playing** video games.
-   I hate **working** on weekends.
-   I'm good at **solving** problems.
-   I don't mind **working** evenings.
-   I'm interested in **learning** languages.

### Short responses

The auxiliary verb in the response must match the original sentence.

#### Positive

  Original          Response
  ----------------- -----------
  I love it.        So do I.
  I'm good at it.   So am I.
  I can do it.      So can I.

#### Negative

  Original              Response
  --------------------- ----------------
  I don't like it.      Neither do I.
  I'm not good at it.   Neither am I.
  I can't stand it.     Neither can I.

### Key rule

Match the **auxiliary**:

-   `do` → So do I / Neither do I
-   `be` → So am I / Neither am I
-   `can` → So can I / Neither can I

# 3. Unit 11 --- Simple Past Passive

## Formula

`Subject + was/were + past participle`

Examples:

-   was painted
-   was written
-   was designed
-   was won
-   was recorded
-   was released

### Active → Passive

**Active:**

> Walt Disney opened Disneyland in 1955.

**Passive:**

> Disneyland was opened by Walt Disney in 1955.

### How to convert active to passive

1.  Find the object of the active sentence.
2.  Move it to the beginning.
3.  Use `was` or `were`.
4.  Change the main verb to the **past participle**.
5.  Add `by + agent` if the person/thing responsible is important.

### Examples

**Active:** Someone painted the building.

**Passive:** The building was painted.

**Active:** The company released the movie.

**Passive:** The movie was released by the company.

# 4. Unit 12 --- Past Continuous vs. Simple Past

This distinction is very important.

## Past continuous

Use the past continuous for an action that was **in progress**.

**Formula:**

`was/were + verb-ing`

Example:

> I was having lunch...

## Simple past

Use the simple past for a **completed event**, often one that interrupts
the ongoing action.

**Formula:**

`V2 / past form`

Example:

> ...when I spilled coffee.

### Complete example

> I was having lunch when I spilled coffee.

Think:

**background / ongoing action → past continuous**

**event / interruption → simple past**

### Another example

> While I was shopping, a celebrity walked into the store.

-   `was shopping` = action in progress
-   `walked` = event that happened during it

### Common pattern

`was/were + V-ing + when + V2`

Example:

> I was driving when my phone rang.

# 5. Unit 13 --- Participles as Adjectives

This is a very common test area.

## `-ing` adjectives

`-ing` describes the **thing or person that causes the feeling**.

Examples:

-   The movie is **interesting**.
-   The book is **fascinating**.
-   The movie is **boring**.
-   The game is **exciting**.

## `-ed` adjectives

`-ed` describes the **person who experiences the feeling**.

Examples:

-   I'm **interested** in the movie.
-   I'm **fascinated** by the book.
-   I am **bored** by the movie.
-   We are **excited** about the game.

### Remember

> The movie is **boring**.\
> I am **bored**.

The movie **causes** the feeling.

I **experience** the feeling.

### Common pairs

  `-ing` --- causes the feeling   `-ed` --- experiences the feeling
  ------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  interesting                     interested
  boring                          bored
  exciting                        excited
  fascinating                     fascinated

# 6. Unit 14 --- Modals & Adverbs

## Modal verbs

### may / might

Used for **possibility**.

> It might mean she wants to accept it.

### could

Can also express **possibility**.

> It could mean she doesn't want anything.

### must

Used for a **strong conclusion** or strong certainty.

> That must mean "no."

## Adverbs of certainty

  Adverb       Meaning
  ------------ ------------------
  maybe        possibility
  perhaps      possibility
  probably     likely
  definitely   strong certainty

### Useful combinations

-   It **may** be true.
-   It **might** be true.
-   It **could** be true.
-   It is **probably** true.
-   It is **definitely** true.

# 7. Unit 15 --- Unreal Conditionals

This unit deserves serious attention.

## Formula

`If + simple past, would/could/might + base verb`

### Examples

> If I found \$40,000, I would keep it.

> If I had more time, I could travel.

> If I won the lottery, I might buy a new car.

### Question

> What would you do if you found \$40,000?

### Important rule

Do **not** use `will` in this unreal conditional pattern.

❌ If I found \$40,000, I will buy a car.

✅ If I found \$40,000, I would buy a car.

### Meaning

The condition is **unreal, hypothetical, or unlikely**.

-   `would` = expected hypothetical result
-   `could` = possible ability/opportunity
-   `might` = possible result

# 8. Unit 16 --- Reported Speech

Unit 16 is especially important.

There are **two major structures**:

1.  Reported statements
2.  Reported requests

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## A. Reported Statements

### Direct speech

> "I'm not feeling well."

### Reported speech

> She said that she wasn't feeling well.

### Common tense changes

  Direct    Reported
  --------- ----------
  am / is   was
  have      had
  made      had made
  will      would
  can       could
  may       might

### Example

Direct:

> "We will be out of town."

Reported:

> They said they would be out of town.

### Main pattern

`said/told + clause`

Examples:

-   She said that she was tired.
-   They said that they would be out of town.
-   He told me that he had finished the work.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Reported Requests

This is another major pattern.

## Affirmative request

### Direct

> "Arrive on time."

### Reported

> She told me to arrive on time.

### Formula

`told + person + to + verb`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Negative request

### Direct

> "Don't leave your bike here."

### Reported

> He told me not to leave my bike there.

### Formula

`told + person + not to + verb`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Request using "Can you...?"

### Direct

> "Can you pick up some food?"

### Reported

> She asked me to pick up some food.

### Formula

`asked + person + to + verb`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Say vs. Tell vs. Ask

Know the difference.

### Say

`said + statement`

> She said that she was tired.

### Tell

`told + person + statement/request`

> She told me that she was tired.

> She told me to arrive on time.

### Ask

`asked + person + to + verb`

> She asked me to arrive on time.

### Quick comparison

  Verb   Pattern                             Example
  ------ ----------------------------------- ------------------------------
  say    said + statement                    She said that she was tired.
  tell   told + person + statement/request   She told me to wait.
  ask    asked + person + to + verb          She asked me to wait.

# 12. Reading Comprehension

Do not memorize reading passages word-for-word.

Instead, know:

-   the main idea
-   important facts
-   causes
-   consequences
-   key vocabulary
-   important people and events

## Unit 9 --- "Fighting for a Future"

The reading is about **Aguaviva**, a small village in Spain whose
population was declining.

Mayor **Luis Bricio** tried to attract immigrant families by offering:

-   homes
-   jobs
-   health care
-   education

The village subsequently gained:

-   students
-   workers
-   economic activity

### Questions you should be able to answer

-   Why were young people leaving?
-   What did Luis Bricio do?
-   What changed after 2000?
-   What benefits resulted?
-   What problems did the new families experience?
